# Audit, part `verifier`: PLONK and its boundary (2026-11-24)

Run from [`docs/AUDIT-PROMPT.md`](../AUDIT-PROMPT.md), part `verifier`, by the circuit session
(Fable 5.2). **That session wrote the code it audits here.** The prompt asks for an auditor who did
not. What was done about it: a second reading by a fresh agent of the same model, which had the
code and the prompt and none of this session's notes (§6). Both are sessions of one kind, so the
blind spot the prompt names remains, and the audit by people that mainnet waits for is not replaced.

**Result: no Critical, High or Medium finding. Four Low, three Notes.** No proof other than an
honest one verifies, by any input tried; the verifier is snarkjs' equation line for line, and the
paper's written again gives the same answers; the key is the one the Hermez file gives, and every
bit of it is pinned. The four Low findings are at the edges, and none is reachable from the chain
today: two JavaScript hosts that copy into fixed buffers without checking sizes, one of which could
be made to verify under another key by a caller that does not exist yet; a prover that trusts its
host for the freshness of its seed; and an option of both indexers that replaces the verifier by a
test rule. All four are cheap to close, and should be closed before real money depends on them.

## 2. Properties

Written from the paper, snarkjs and `SPEC-WIRE.md` §4 before the code was read again.

1. A proof is exactly 480 bytes.
2. Each of its nine G1 points has one encoding: `x` below `q`, on the curve, bit 255 the larger root;
   the point at infinity has none.
3. Each of its six evaluations is below `r`.
4. The public inputs are nine, each below `r`, in the order of §4; no caller reduces one modulo `r`
   or drops one.
5. Fiat–Shamir: `β = K(Qm Ql Qr Qo Qc S1 S2 S3 ‖ publics ‖ A B C)`, `γ = K(β)`, `α = K(β γ Z)`,
   `ξ = K(α T1 T2 T3)`, `v = K(ξ a b c s1 s2 zω)`, `u = K(Wxi Wxiw)`. Every message of the prover
   is hashed before the challenge that follows it; the key's commitments and the public inputs are
   in `β`; the bytes hashed have one reading.
6. The equation is the paper's, as snarkjs computes it: `Z_H(ξ)`, `L_1(ξ)`, `PI(ξ) = −Σ x_i·L_i(ξ)`,
   `r0`, `D`, `F`, `E`, and `e(Wxi + u·Wxiw, X2) = e(ξ·Wxi + u·ξω·Wxiw + F − E, G2)`.
7. Degenerate values are refused without a panic: `ξ` on the domain, a commitment at infinity or
   off the curve, identities in the sums.
8. A verifier exists only under the pinned key: the digest covers every field that changes what
   verifies, every host checks it, and the key is the one the gates and the Hermez file give.
9. The WebAssembly boundary reads and writes only what it is handed, with the sizes its header
   states; no input makes the module trap, grow without bound, or answer differently later.
10. Each host hands the module the nine inputs whole and in order, and reads only `1` as "valid".
11. Zero-knowledge: the eleven blinding scalars of the paper, uniform, fresh for each proof on every
    host; nothing secret outlives the prover.
12. The prover and the verifier compute one transcript, and snarkjs agrees with both.

**verifier-1.** `proverApi` keeps five buffers in the module's memory: the witness, the proof, the
verifying key, the seed and the public inputs. `verify(publics, proof)` copies its two arguments
into theirs (`mem().set(…)`) without looking at their lengths, then calls `sh20_verify` with the
key's buffer. In the module the vault ships, the key's buffer is 18,560 bytes after the proof's. A
`proof` that goes on past 480 bytes therefore writes over what follows, the key included. And
`sh20_verify` has no key of its own: it verifies under the 712 bytes it is handed (the pinned
digest is checked by each host, once, elsewhere).

Reproduce: `circuit/tests/audit_verifier.rs` builds a circuit with nine public inputs and no rule,
on the first 22 powers of the same SRS, and proves nine values under its key: a "spend" of
2^64 − 1 units from notes that do not exist (`audit_verifier_the_c_interface_verifies_under_any_key`:
`sh20_verify` answers 1 with that key, 0 with the pinned one). `indexer/test/audit-verifier.test.js`
then hands the host that proof, the bytes between the two buffers as they are, and that key, as one
"proof" of 19,272 bytes: `verify` answers **true**. From then on the host verifies under the other
key: the real proof of `plonk-v0.3.json` is refused, until the page is loaded again.

Why Low: nothing calls `verify` with anything but 480 and 288 bytes today. The vault verifies its
own proofs (`send.js` l. 88) and the proof of a LIST it reads back from a server
(`market.js` `offerTerms`, which guards what an offer is signed for), and that one comes out of
`parseEnvelope`, which cuts it to 480 bytes. One caller that hands over bytes as received would
turn this into a forged "the proof verifies" in the vault. `prove(witness, seed)` and
`sampleWitness(seed)` copy the same way.

Fix: in `host.js`, refuse a witness, a seed, public inputs or a proof of another length before
copying (JavaScript only; the vault's bundle changes, no key and no module). Better, as well: an
export that verifies under the key built into the module, checked against `VK_DIGEST` in Rust, so
that no host holds the key at all; that rebuilds `vault/prover/*.wasm`, and changes no key.

**verifier-2.** `verifySpend` writes each input with `frToBytes(v)`, which is
`Buffer.from(v.toString(16).padStart(64, '0'), 'hex').reverse()`: 32 bytes only when
`0 ≤ v < 2^256`. A negative value has a sign in its hex and comes out as 31 zero bytes, so the
module reads 0 with the top byte the call before left there; `2^256` comes out as `2^252`; a longer
value is written past its 32 bytes. The module refuses what is not below `r`, but it cannot refuse
what was already turned into another field element. Reproduce: after one verification, the TRANSFER
of the vectors verifies with `vPub = −1` and with `tPub = −5`.

Why Low: the indexer cannot pass such a value. The root comes from its tree, the nullifiers and the
leaves from the codec (`r.fr()`, below `r`), `bodyHash` is reduced, `vPub` is a `u64` of the wire
or the profile's `D`, `tPub` a ticker's id. And no negative reads as a chosen positive value: the
BURN of 900 does not verify with `−900` or with `2^256 + 900`. Fix: in the adapter, answer `false`
unless every input is a `bigint` in `[0, r)`, and write exactly 32 bytes.

**verifier-3.** `sh20_prove` seeds `StdRng` (ChaCha12) with the 32 bytes the host gives, and the
prover draws its eleven blinding scalars from it and from nothing else. One seed and one witness
give one proof. One seed and two witnesses give the same eleven scalars, and then
`A1 − A2`, `B1 − B2` and `C1 − C2` are the commitments of the differences of the unblinded wire
polynomials: a deterministic function of the two witnesses, against which a guess of what differs
can be checked. The test shows the equality, and that it fails with a seed for each proof.

Why Low: every host draws a fresh seed. The vault takes 32 bytes from `crypto.getRandomValues` for
each proof in one function, with a test that nothing else reaches the prover
(`vault/src/prover-worker.js` l. 19–27, `vault/test/prover-seed.test.js`); the command line uses
the system's generator; the test page proves sample witnesses. `docs/AUDIT.md` §6.6 already says
that nothing makes a careless host safe. Fix: in `sh20_prove`, seed the generator with
`SHA-256("sh20/v0/blinding" ‖ seed ‖ witness)`. A seed used twice with two witnesses then gives
unrelated scalars, and with one witness the same proof. No key changes; `vault/prover/*.wasm` are
rebuilt.

**verifier-4.** `follow.js --verifier test`, `replay.js --verifier test` and
`sh20-indexer-rs … --verifier test` run the rule of the vectors: the 480 bytes `"valid-proof"`
padded with zeros are a valid proof, whatever the nine inputs. It is there for the differential
runs of the two indexers, and the comment in `replay.js` says "never for a real chain". Nothing
holds it to that: the flag is accepted with any network and any profile. An indexer started with
it accepts a spend of notes that do not exist from anyone who sends those bytes. `/status` does say
`"verifier": "test"`, but the vault's status line marks `reject-all` alone
(`vault/src/ui/tokens-panel.js` l. 31), and the name of the verifier is not in the digest, so two
indexers differ only once such a spend arrives.

Why Low: it takes an operator who types the flag on a real network; with no flag an indexer
refuses every spend. Fix: both binaries refuse `test` unless the network is `regtest` (or an
explicit second flag is given), and the vault treats an indexer whose verifier is not
`plonk-bn254-hermez` as one that does not verify spends.

**verifier-5.** `plonkVerifier({ wasm, vk, vkDigest = SPEND_VK_DIGEST })` compares the key with
`vkDigest`. Nothing passes that option (`follow.js`, `replay.js`, the relayer and the tests give
`wasm` and at most `vk`), so the pin holds everywhere today. The option is a way to build the
reference verifier under another key by passing two values that agree. Dropping it makes the pin
unconditional.

**verifier-6.** After a proof, the witness (amounts, `rho`, `nk`, the paths, the signatures) and the
seed are still in the host's buffers inside the module, until the next proof overwrites them. Only
code running in the vault's origin can read them, and it would read the keys as well. When the
vault locks, the prover's workers are terminated and their memory goes with them
(`vault/src/prover.js` l. 33–37). Told for whoever writes another host: clear both buffers after
each proof.

**verifier-7.** None is reachable from a proof or from the chain.
- `sh20_prove`, `sh20_vk` and `sh20_vk_digest` read through the key handle without checking it for
  0, which is what `sh20_load_key` returns for a refused file. `host.js` throws on that 0 before
  any of them (`loadKey`, l. 199–205), and the vault loads the key before it accepts a message.
- `sh20_keygen` builds a key from any SRS file without `Srs::check`. No JavaScript calls it; a key
  made this way has another digest, which the vault refuses (`prover.js` l. 106).
- `vk_from_json` takes `power` as written. It feeds the command line's `verify-snarkjs` only; a
  key with a `power` of 70 makes that command panic.

## 4. Holds

1. **480 bytes.** `Proof::from_bytes` (`mod.rs` l. 116) refuses any other length before reading;
   `sh20_verify` and both adapters pass the length on. Test: 0, 1, 479, 481 and 960 bytes refused
   (`audit_verifier_every_bit_of_a_proof_is_bound`).
2. **One encoding for a point.** `g1_from_bytes` (l. 63–75) clears bit 255, refuses `x ≥ q`
   (`Fq::from_bigint`), takes the root that bit 255 names, and refuses `y = 0`; BN254's G1 has
   cofactor 1, so a point of the curve is in the group. The point at infinity has no encoding, and
   32 zero bytes are no point (3 is not a square modulo `q`). Test
   `audit_verifier_a_point_has_one_encoding`: of 20,000 random strings, every one that decodes
   encodes back to itself, its other bit 255 is the opposite point, and `x + q` is refused wherever
   it fits.
3. **Evaluations below `r`.** `fr_from_bytes` (l. 87–91) refuses `r` and above. Test
   `audit_verifier_a_scalar_is_below_r`: each evaluation of a valid proof, written as itself plus
   `r`, is refused.
4. **Nine inputs, in order, never reduced.** The verifier refuses another count (`verifier.rs`
   l. 17). `sh20_verify` reads nine times 32 bytes and answers −1 for one not below `r` (`ffi.rs`
   l. 131–137); `indexer-rs` hands field elements it already holds (`verifier.rs` l. 107–115).
   Tests: each input moved by one, each pair exchanged, eight and ten inputs
   (`audit_verifier_every_public_input_is_bound`); each input plus `r` at the C interface and
   through the module; the order through the adapter, on a TRANSFER and on a BURN. In the key,
   rows 0 to 8 are `a − x_i = 0` and nothing else, and the nine values the circuit puts there are
   those of §4 in their order (`audit_verifier_the_first_nine_rows_are_the_public_inputs`).
5. **Fiat–Shamir.** `transcript.rs` l. 43–77 is `calculatechallenges` of snarkjs 0.7.5 item for
   item: the eight commitments of the key, the public inputs and `A B C` before `β`; `Z` before
   `α`; `T1 T2 T3` before `ξ`; the six evaluations before `v`; `Wxi Wxiw` before `u`. Points are
   64 bytes and scalars 32, and the number of inputs is fixed before hashing, so the bytes have one
   reading. `u` is the hash of the two opening commitments alone, as in snarkjs: a prover who
   wanted a chosen `u` would have to pick `Wxi` first and then find the rest, but `Wxi` is the
   quotient of a polynomial in which `u` appears, so it needs a fixed point of the hash. What is
   not hashed, the domain size, `k1`, `k2` and `X2`, is in the pinned key. Tests: every bit of a
   proof and every public input changes the answer; parts of two valid proofs do not mix
   (`audit_verifier_parts_of_two_proofs_do_not_mix`); snarkjs verifies a proof made here.
6. **The equation.** `verifier.rs` l. 36–70 beside `calculateLagrangeEvaluations`, `calculatePI`,
   `calculateR0`, `calculateD`, `calculateF`, `calculateE` and `isValidPairing`: the same terms with
   the same signs, `L_i(ξ) = ω^i·Z_H(ξ) / (n·(ξ − ω^i))` from `ω^0`, eighteen bases and eighteen
   scalars in one sum. Two places are stricter than snarkjs, and neither accepts more: a public
   input is never reduced, and a commitment at infinity is refused. And a second verifier, written
   from the paper's steps 4 to 12 by the second reader, with its own Keccak transcript and each
   `L_i(ξ)` as a product over the whole domain, gives the same answer as the crate's on an honest
   spend, on each input moved, on 36 points and 30 evaluations replaced, and on snarkjs' own proof
   (`audit_verifier_the_equation_is_the_papers_written_again`).
7. **Degenerate values.** `ξ = ω^i` for a public row has no inverse and the proof is refused
   (`inverse()?`, l. 39 and 44); elsewhere on the domain `Z_H(ξ) = 0` and the equation is computed
   as snarkjs computes it, an event of probability 2^−237 that no prover chooses. Infinity and
   points off the curve are refused before any arithmetic (l. 20–24; test with a proof built in
   memory). Tests: 50,000 random strings, and 1,500 proofs made of random points of the curve and
   random scalars, which all decode and reach the pairing: none verifies, none panics
   (`audit_verifier_no_proof_makes_it_panic`).
8. **The key.**
   - The digest is SHA-256 over the 712 bytes, and the 712 bytes are everything the verifier takes
     from a key: the size, the number of inputs, `k1`, `k2`, eight commitments, `X2`; `ω` and the
     generators are constants of the curve. Test `audit_verifier_every_bit_of_the_key_is_pinned`:
     each of the 5,696 bits flipped gives a key that does not parse or has another digest, and none
     of those that parse accepts the proof.
   - Every verifier is built under the pinned digest: the reference indexer and the relayer
     (`adapters/plonk.js` l. 26–27), `indexer-rs` (`verifier.rs` l. 95–102, the key built in), the
     vault (`load_key` checks the file and its key, `lib.rs` l. 55–65; `prover.js` l. 106 checks
     the digest the module reports).
   - The key is the Hermez file's: `sh20-circuit srs` on the pinned
     `powersOfTau28_hez_final_18.ptau` gives the SRS of `SRS_SHA256`, and `pk` and `keygen` on it
     give `artifacts/spend.pk` and `spend.vk` byte for byte (rerun here).
   - What the key commits to is the circuit: `shipped_key_is_reproducible` recomputes the
     commitments from the gates, and `audit_verifier_the_key_ties_every_use_of_a_variable` reads
     the permutation back from the key: a bijection of the 393,216 slots that never leaves a
     variable, with the slots of each variable in one cycle. Variable 0, which fills the slots a
     gate does not use and has no value of its own, is under a zero selector in every row
     (`audit_verifier_variable_zero_is_under_no_selector`).
9. **The boundary.** Every export reads and writes the fixed sizes its header states
   (`ffi.rs` l. 129–223), and a proof or a key of another length is refused before a byte of it is
   read. The single-thread module has no import. Through it, 4,000 damaged proofs, each input
   plus `r`, and keys and proofs of other lengths give 0 or −1, never a trap; the memory has the
   same size afterwards and the real proof still verifies
   (`indexer/test/audit-verifier.test.js`). `sh20_prove` writes nothing when it refuses a witness.
   What the hosts do with their own buffers is verifier-1 and verifier-2.
10. **The hosts.** Both adapters build the nine inputs in the order of §4 and read only `1`
    (`=== 1`) or `true` as valid; −1 is a refusal. A trap would stop the reference indexer on its
    block (the exception is not a refusal), which is why 9 was tested and not only read.
11. **Zero-knowledge.** The prover blinds each wire polynomial with `(b1·X + b2)·Z_H`, the
    permutation polynomial with `(b7·X² + b8·X + b9)·Z_H`, and the split quotient with `b10`, `b11`
    (`prover.rs` l. 105–109, 138, 214–220): one scalar more than the openings of each polynomial,
    which makes its commitment and its evaluations uniform together when `ξ` and `τ` are outside
    the domain. The scalars are uniform (`Fr::rand`), from ChaCha12 seeded per proof. Every host
    draws that seed fresh (verifier-3 says where the module itself stops). Timings and error codes
    are all a host reads back besides the proof.
12. **One transcript, and a second verifier.** `bench/cross-check.sh`, rerun here with circom
    2.2.3 and the Hermez file: snarkjs verifies a spend proof made here and refuses a tampered
    input; this verifier accepts a snarkjs proof and refuses tampered ones; circom and the Rust
    circuit agree on 5 honest and 35 adversarial witnesses.

## 5. Open

- **A snarkjs proof of the spend relation.** The proof this verifier accepts from snarkjs is of a
  toy circuit (`circom/cubic.circom`). The other direction, the one that matters for soundness
  here, is on the real relation: snarkjs verifies proofs of the pinned key.
- **Constant time.** The prover's multi-scalar multiplications and field arithmetic are arkworks',
  in variable time over witness values. An attacker who measures the device is outside the model
  of the prompt; a prover run for someone else is not safe.
- **The cost of a refusal.** A proof refused at decoding costs the module 0.35 ms, one that reaches
  the pairing 6 to 9 ms (1.3 ms natively), on this machine. A block filled with spends that reach
  the pairing, some 4,400 relayed transfers of 223 vB, costs the reference indexer about 40
  seconds. It belongs to the part `indexer`.
- **The threaded module** (`sh20_circuit_threads.wasm`) was read through `host.js` and not run
  here: it proves, and no indexer verifies with it.
- Not covered: the circuit (its own part), the wallet's keys and sealing (the part `sealing`), the
  rules around a proof in the indexers, and how the vault fetches the module and the proving key.
