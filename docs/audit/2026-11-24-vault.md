# Audit, part `vault`: the browser wallet (2026-11-24)

Run from [`docs/AUDIT-PROMPT.md`](../AUDIT-PROMPT.md), part `vault`, by a session of its own on
Fable 5.2, the model the prompt asks for on this part.

**Result: no Critical finding. One High, two Medium, ten Low, four Notes.** The vault's keys, its
signatures and what it has an external wallet sign hold as the specs and the README say (§4), and
the nine fixes of `docs/AUDIT.md` §8 are in the code as described. What does not hold is around
them: the vault pays for a listing or a ticker one block old (vault-1), it has no way back for a
spend that is never mined, which anyone can bring about (vault-2), and its state costs more at
every sync the more notes it holds, which anyone can add to (vault-3).

vault-1 to vault-13 are the audit of the commit named in §1. vault-14 to vault-17 come from a
second reading, of what `main` took in while the audit ran: the mainnet profile (#85) first, which

## 2. Properties

Written from the specs and the README before the code was read.

Keys and storage

1. The 12 words come from 128 fresh random bits; a phrase with an unknown word or a wrong checksum
   derives nothing; `m` is that of `SPEC-NOTES.md` §1.
2. Every note key, the address, a nullifier and a spend's signature are computed by `circuit/js`,
   and a secret goes through WebCrypto wherever the platform has the primitive.
3. The stored record and the backup are AES-256-GCM under a PBKDF2 key, with a fresh nonce at each
   write; no backup is written in clear; a wrong passphrase opens nothing.
4. A lock takes the secrets out of the page; a stale copy of the vault never writes over a newer
   one.
5. Counters never repeat: a public-note index and a lock index are saved before they are used, and a
   restored vault checks both against the chain first.

Paying and receiving

6. A payer makes the five checks of `SPEC-NOTES.md` §2 on an address, for the page's network.
7. Each output is sealed with a fresh ephemeral key; unused outputs go to the vault itself; the
   order of the outputs says nothing; an unused input has fresh random keys.
8. Scanning follows `SPEC-NOTES.md` §4: a low-order `pk_eph` is skipped from its bytes, a failure is
   an error and not "not ours", the ticker check, the leaf check, notes told apart by position, a
   note of 0 kept out.
9. A public note counts only if `(ticker, v, npk, 0)` hashes to the leaf at its position, in a tree
   whose root is the indexer's.
10. The vault never asks an indexer about one of its own notes or listings.

Spends

11. A spend's inputs open with the vault's keys to their leaves; amounts balance; the body signed is
    every byte before the proof; the envelope published is the one proved.
12. The prover gets a fresh seed for each proof, and the proof is checked against its public inputs
    before anything is signed.
13. What the vault sends, the indexers can accept: the anchor is a root they know, inside the window
    when the spend confirms, and the terms pass the rules of `SPEC-WIRE.md` §5 at the block they
    land in.
14. The key of a reveal is saved before the wallet signs the commit; the notes of a spend come back
    when the spend can no longer be accepted, and only then.

Bitcoin transactions and the external wallet

15. The wallet signs only transactions the vault built, and the vault sends a signed transaction
    only if its inputs, outputs, sequences and locktime are the ones it built.
16. The vault's own signatures are BIP340, over BIP341 messages (key path and script path; the
    default mode, and `SINGLE | ANYONECANPAY` for an offer), by keys only it holds; neither a wallet
    nor a server can have them sign anything else.
17. Each of its transactions carries exactly one envelope where the indexers look for one.
18. Fees are bounded: a server's suggestion is capped, a rate above 1,000 sat/vB refused, a fee
    above 20,000 sats asked about.

The sale

19. A LIST names its lock key in the signed body; the lock is a key-path output of a key only the
    vault holds, used once.
20. An offer binds the lock's outpoint, amount and script and the output that pays the price; the
    seller signs only terms it can vouch for.
21. A buyer pays only for a listing that exists, the price and the payee the seller signed, and
    receives the tokens; a seller is paid whenever the lock is spent through the offer.
22. A CLAIM is signed for the listing's txid and a note key of the vault.

Servers

23. The profile is the vault's own; an indexer on another profile stops every action.
24. What an indexer says is checked against the chain where the chain can show it (the terms of a
    DEPLOY, of a LIST), and against the other indexers where it cannot.
25. No answer of a server and no note sent to the vault stops the page, and none reaches the DOM as
    markup.

The page

26. No script from elsewhere runs: the content security policy, the import map, the refusal of
    frames, of path gateways, and of servers named in a link without a confirmation.
27. The service worker is registered only where the origin is the vault's alone, and only adds the
    isolation headers.
28. A release is the commit's files, byte for byte, and the prover modules are what `circuit/`
    builds.

The mainnet profile (written from the description of its commit, before its changes were read)

29. Every address the page reads, shows or pays is of the profile's network, Bitcoin's and SH-20's;
    one of another network is refused.
30. A wallet is asked for the profile's network, and is held to it by the address it gives, not by
    its word.
31. A vault's records are those of one network and are never read as another's; a user of one
    network is not left without a way to the other.
32. Nothing is read or done without an indexer the user named, on the profile's network.
33. What was left loose because signet coins are worth nothing is not left loose on mainnet: the
    coins the vault spends, the fees it proposes, the page it is served as.

**vault-1.** A TAKE pays the seller at the level of Bitcoin: the offer's signature needs only the
lock output to exist. Whether it buys anything is the indexers' decision, in the chain the TAKE
confirms in: the listing must be ACTIVE there. `sendTake` requires two indexers to report the
listing ACTIVE now, and asks nothing about its age: not the listing's `height`, which `normListing`
keeps (`api.js` l. 75), nor how deep the LIST transaction is. The regtest run buys a listing as soon
as the indexers report it. A reorg of the LIST's block can leave the LIST transaction in the chain
with its envelope refused, in two ways.

- A seller who wants it holds a second spend of the same notes, a TRANSFER to itself. In the new
  chain it is mined before the LIST (the same block, a higher fee): the LIST is refused ("nullifier
  already spent"), and its transaction still creates the lock output. One block of reorg is enough.
- With nobody wanting it, a reorg that reaches the LIST's anchor block and changes the tree there
  (the same mints in another order) gives another `R[anchorHeight]`: the proof no longer verifies,
  and the lock output is there all the same.

In both, the buyer's TAKE is a valid transaction, whether the buyer's broadcast reaches the new
chain or the seller keeps it and sends it again: it pays the price to the payee, and the indexers
answer "TAKE must spend an active listing lock". There is no listing, so nothing can be claimed
back. The seller has the price and the tokens. With the signet's default indexers the window is
wider than a block: the mirrors run 10 to 30 minutes behind, and show a LIST of an abandoned block
as ACTIVE until their next build.

A MINT with payMode 2 has the same shape, with the deployer in the seller's place. `verifyDeploy`
asks that the DEPLOY be confirmed, not how deep, and a DEPLOY may open its mint in the block after
its own (`start = H + 1`). If a reorg moves the DEPLOY one block later it is refused (`start > H`
no longer holds), and the mints sent meanwhile pay `payScript` for a ticker that does not exist.
With payMode 1 the sats are burned for nothing, and nobody gains.

High by the table: BTC is taken, under a reorg, which the prompt gives the attacker. What it takes
outside the table: the block of the LIST, or of the DEPLOY, has to be replaced, by mining or by one
of the one-block reorgs the network has from time to time, while a buyer or a minter acts on it.

Reproduce: `vault-1: a TAKE of a listing one block old…` builds the LIST, the offer and the TAKE
with the vault's own functions, passes `listingCheck` with two indexers, then applies the other
chain to the reference indexer: the TRANSFER is accepted, the LIST and the TAKE refused, no listing,
no note, and output 0 of the TAKE pays the seller 5,000 sats. `vault-1: a MINT paid to the
deployer…` does the same for a DEPLOY that `deployMismatches` and `mintProblems` accept. The second
way above (another root at the anchor) follows from `SPEC-WIRE.md` §5, "root known … proof valid,
with the nine inputs"; it was not run, since it takes a real proof.

Fix: depth before payment. The vault waits six blocks elsewhere (`REORG_MARGIN`, `REFUSAL_DEPTH`).
Buy stays off until the listing is six blocks deep at every indexer that answers
(`status.height − listing.height + 1 ≥ 6`) and its transaction six deep at Esplora, with the count
on the page; a MINT of a deployed ticker that pays or burns waits until `deployHeight` is six deep.
SH20 has no DEPLOY, and a free mint risks its fee alone: neither needs to wait. The README's
"a sale can be neither doubled nor left unpaid" holds for the seller; it should say what the buyer
relies on.

**vault-2.** `addPending` marks a spend's notes `spending` before the wallet is asked to sign
(`keys.js` l. 424, called at `ui/spends.js` l. 76). They come back in three cases: a DEPLOY whose
ticker was taken, a DEPLOY proved again, and a reveal confirmed six blocks deep with none of its
nullifiers recorded (`refusedSpend`, which answers false as long as the reveal has no height).
Nothing covers a spend that is never mined:

- the wallet does not sign (the user closes its window): the record stays `prepared`, a status the
  list of spends leaves out (`ui/send-panel.js` l. 149);
- the commit or the reveal is dropped or replaced: "Broadcast again" sends the same two
  transactions, which the network now refuses.

A sync, a rescan from zero, a lock and an unlock leave the notes `spending`: `matchPublic` skips
that status (`match.js` l. 60) and `receive` skips a position it knows. The notes are unspent on
chain. In this vault they are out of the balance and out of `pickInputs`, and the way back is to
erase the vault and restore it from its words, which the page does not say.

Anyone can cause the second case. With Xverse and Leather the list of coins is Esplora's as it
comes, unconfirmed ones included (`api.js` l. 246 records `confirmed`; nothing reads it); UniSat's
own list is taken when it gives one, and marked confirmed without a check (`wallets.js` l. 103).
`selectCoins` takes the smallest coin that pays everything. The wallet's address is public: it
funds each commit and takes each reveal's change. The attacker pays it a few thousand sats and
leaves that payment unconfirmed; the next commit spends that coin; the attacker replaces its
payment, and the commit and the reveal leave every mempool with it. The cost is the fee of the
replacement, which has to exceed those of the transactions it evicts. A vault that holds one or
two notes of a ticker, as after a mint, has that balance frozen.

Reproduce: `vault-2: …`: `selectCoins` picks the unconfirmed coin; after `addPending` and a wallet
that does not sign, the note is `spending`, no spend is listed, a full match leaves it so; after a
broadcast whose reveal never gets a height, `refusedSpend` is false thousands of blocks later.

Fix, in three parts. Fund from confirmed coins, and say so when there are none. Give the notes
back when the spend can no longer be accepted: its envelope's `anchorHeight` is more than `W`
blocks below the synced tip, so no block takes it, in this transaction or in a copy
(`SPEC-WIRE.md` §5), and every indexer lists none of its nullifiers (`refusalAgreed`); a record
still `prepared` showed its envelope to nobody and can be given back at once. List the `prepared`
records, with a way to give them up.

**vault-3.** The same finding as sealing-3 of `docs/audit/2026-11-24-sealing.md`, reached there from
the sender's side: one fix closes both, and two tests turn round with it (`vault-3: …` here,
`sealing-3: …` in `vault/test/audit-sealing.test.js`). What this report adds to it is the vault's
own spends, below, which fill the same record with no sender at all. `checkOwned` calls `updateNote`
for every note the vault holds, spent ones included, at every sync. `updateNote` saves, and a save
is the whole state through `JSON.stringify`, AES-GCM, base64 and `localStorage.setItem`. `N` notes
cost `N` writes of a state `N` notes long. Measured with the vault's own classes on Node 22, an
Apple M1 Pro, an encrypted vault and a storage kept in memory, so without what a browser adds for
the write itself:

| Notes held | Stored record | One sync's writes |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | 77 KiB | 0.1 s |
| 250 | 193 KiB | 0.7 s |
| 500 | 385 KiB | 4.2 s |
| 1,000 | 771 KiB | 27 s |
| 2,000 | 1,544 KiB | 113 s |

Nothing leaves the state: a spent note stays, and so does the record of every spend, with its
envelope and its two transactions (6,048 characters stored for a TRANSFER, measured). A sealed
note takes 789 characters of the record. `localStorage` holds about five million for an origin:
some 6,000 notes or 800 spends fill it, after which `save` throws, the vault cannot record the
one-time key of a spend, and so cannot spend.

Anyone can add notes to a vault. A TRANSFER has two inputs and three outputs, so each one leaves
up to two notes more than it spent: a thousand notes of one unit take about 500 transfers, each
about 490 vB alone (a reveal of 339 vB and a commit of about 150) or 223 vB in a batch of ten
(`CLAUDE.md`, the cost budget). That is 110,000 to 245,000 vB for their sender, and each sync of
the vault that receives them then takes half a minute before the browser's own share; the victim
merges them two per spend at best. An address is meant to be published and used again
(`SPEC-NOTES.md` §2).

Reproduce: `vault-3: …` counts one write of the whole state for each of 300 notes. The table is
that loop with a passphrase set, timed for each `N`; sealing-3 has 40 s at 1,000 notes on its
machine.

Fix: one save for a sync, with every change in it; `pathAt` and `spent` are computed again at
every sync and need not be in the saved state at all. Then let the state shrink: a spent note and
the transactions of a mined spend are of no use once six blocks deep. If it must still grow, the
cache's IndexedDB has room that `localStorage` has not.

**vault-4.** `deployProblems` compares `start` with the next block, and its test applies the
DEPLOY to the reference indexer at that height. The vault never puts one there: the reveal waits
until the commit is `N = 6` blocks deep, so it lands at `tip + 1 + N` at the earliest, and later
when the commit is slow to confirm. The indexers want `start > H` at the reveal's block. A `start`
up to `tip + N + 1` passes the screen and cannot pass the chain; with the form's own default,
`tip + 12`, a commit that waits five blocks for its confirmation ends the same way. `processDeploys`
and `deploySchedule` look at the commit's depth and at the proof's anchor, never at `start`, so
the reveal goes out all the same. Its fees are lost, nothing is burned, and the ticker and its
terms are in the open before any DEPLOY holds them, which the maturity rule was there to prevent.

Reproduce: `vault-4: …`: no problem found for `start = tip + 2`, the schedule says "ready", and the
reference indexer with `N = 6` refuses the reveal at its first possible block ("start must be after
the DEPLOY block").

Fix: the screen asks `start > nextHeight + N` and says why; `processDeploys` holds a reveal whose
`start ≤ tip + 1` and marks it as it marks an expired anchor, to be deployed again with a later
start.

**vault-5.** `FIELDS` holds every term of a DEPLOY but `unlock`. The tickers table shows the team
allocation as `pre @ unlock` (`ui/tokens-panel.js` l. 91). With one indexer, or several that agree,
half of a supply shown as locked for decades can be spendable at the first block of the mint, and
the check the mint screen describes as "against the DEPLOY transaction on chain" does not see it.
Hard requirement 3 of `CLAUDE.md` is about that number. Reproduce: `vault-5: …`. Fix: `unlock` in
`FIELDS`.

**vault-6.** A DEPLOY, always, and a LIST are in the witness of input 0. `deployMismatches` and
`listingFromChain` recompute the txid of what Esplora returns, then read the envelope from the
witness, which a txid does not cover: `docs/AUDIT.md` §8.2 found it for the LIST, and its fix covers
the offer. For a mint, an indexer and an Esplora that say the same wrong thing, a price paid to a
script of theirs, pass the check. For an offer after a restore, the same pair brings the vault to
its confirmation dialog, where the seller's reading of a price and an address is the only check
left.

BIP341 has the missing link. The tapscript of the witness, hashed as a leaf and combined with the
internal key of its control block, must give the key of the output spent; that output is in the
transaction the outpoint names, and both txids cover their side. Fetching the transaction that
input 0 spends and making that one check binds every byte of the envelope to the txid. Esplora can
then only withhold, the DEPLOY check no longer needs it to be honest, and a listing found again
after a restore is vouched for by the chain, without a proof and without a question.

Reproduce: `vault-6: …`: the same transaction with another `payScript` in its envelope has the same
txid and passes `deployMismatches` with an indexer that says the same; the output key computed from
the real witness is the commit's, the one from the forged witness is not. Fix: that check, in one
function that `deploy.js` and `market.js` both call.

**vault-7.** `#checkUnchanged` refuses to write over a record whose salt changed, which catches a
passphrase set or changed elsewhere. A write that keeps the passphrase is not seen. The page locks
a tab when it hears another one write (`app.js` l. 77), and that closes most of it. What is left is
a save under way in the other tab: between the check and `setItem`, an encrypted vault awaits its
AES-GCM, and a sync makes one such save for each note (vault-3), seconds of them on a vault of a
few hundred. The stale write then locks the tab that was ahead as well. What is lost is an index
just reserved, and the next MINT or TAKE uses that public-note key again (two notes linked,
`SPEC-NOTES.md` §5); or a spend's record with its one-time key: the sats of its commit output, and
for a DEPLOY that waits for its maturity, the reveal. The README says a tab "never writes its older
copy back".

Reproduce: `vault-7: …`, with two `Vault` objects on one storage: both reserve index 0 and the same
`npk`, and the second write drops the pending record of the first. This is `keys.js` alone; the
page's listener is not in the test, and how often a browser lets the stale write through was not
measured.

Fix: keep the text last read or written and refuse to save over another one, compared just before
`setItem`; or hold a Web Lock around each read, change and write.

**vault-8.** `addListing` names a record `list:<lockIndex>`. `matchListings` adds any listing of
the indexers' lists whose lock key is the vault's and whose txid it does not know. Anyone can make
a second listing with one of the vault's keys: by listing tokens of their own to a key seen on
chain (a gift, and a row in the victim's panel), or by copying the victim's LIST from the mempool
into a transaction that pays 330 sats to the same key and confirms first, so that the listing
lives under the copier's txid and the victim's own transaction is refused. `syncListings` then
moves each record to its listing's state through `updateListing(rec.id, …)`
(`ui/market-panel.js` l. 34), which changes the first record of that id: the second listing's state
lands on the first, and the second keeps the state it was found with. After a copy, the row that
is no listing reads SOLD and the real one ACTIVE for ever. The vault can still sell or cancel the
real listing, through the proof path of `offerTerms`; that was read, not run.

Reproduce: `vault-8: …`. Fix: the id from `listTxid`; and when a record the vault sent is no
listing while another listing carries its lock key, say that another transaction published it.

**vault-9.** The same finding as sealing-8 of the sealing audit; one fix closes both, and two
tests turn round (`vault-9: …` here, `sealing-8: …` in `vault/test/audit-sealing.test.js`).
`receive` answers null for a position it has a record for, before it looks at the
leaf. `checkOwned` then drops a sealed record whose leaf is no longer at its position, "scanning
finds it again wherever it now is"; but the scan ran first, in the same sync. A later sync reads
those leaves again only while their block is among the last six (`sync.js` l. 114), and the page
syncs at an unlock and on demand. After that the two notes are back only with "Rescan from zero".
Nothing is lost on chain; the balance is short until then.

Reproduce: `vault-9: …` shows `receive` refusing both notes at their swapped positions; the drop
and the six blocks are read in the code. Fix: in `receive`, a record at that position with another
leaf is replaced; or drop before matching.

**vault-10.** `mintProblems` divides by `token.lim`, and so does `renderTokens`
(`ui/tokens-panel.js` l. 88), inside `renderAll`, after the vault and wallet panels and before the
four others. A primary indexer that lists one such token leaves the notes, send and market panels

**vault-11.** `pages.yml` (l. 21) and `CLAUDE.md` say the vault isolates itself on the Pages site

**vault-12.** Esplora is not in the prompt's list of attackers, and it is the vault's third
server. Read as untrusted: its fee suggestion (capped at 200 sat/vB), the value of a coin (every

- the tip. `tipHeight` is `Number(text)`; with an answer that is not a number, `anchor` skips both

- the height of a DEPLOY's commit. An Esplora that says "six deep" early is handed the reveal, and

indexers report (they follow the chain themselves), and hold a reveal while the two are far apart.

**vault-13.** `docs/AUDIT.md` §8.3 leaves that two indexers run by one party count as two. Two

**vault-14.** `Vault.open` refuses a record of another network with "this vault was made on

worth money.

**vault-15.** `selectCoins` takes the smallest coin that pays everything. On signet a coin is a

## 4. Holds

By property; the line numbers are those of the commit.

1. **Words.** `newMnemonic` draws 16 bytes from `crypto.getRandomValues` (`bip39.js` l. 38);
   `mnemonicToSeed` calls `mnemonicToEntropy` first, which refuses a count, a word or a checksum
   (l. 23 to 33, l. 42); the seed is WebCrypto's PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512, 2,048 rounds, and `m` is
   `masterSecret` of `circuit/js` (`keys.js` l. 148). `test/bip39.test.js`, `test/keys.test.js`.
2. **Keys.** `notekeys.js` computes nothing: `walletKeys`, `publicOpening`, `scan`, `nullifier`,
   `encodeAddress` are `circuit/js/webcrypto.js`, whose X25519, HKDF, AES-GCM, HMAC and the EdDSA
   nonce's SHA-256 go through `subtle.js`. The `node:crypto` stand-in hashes with SHA-256 and throws
   for the rest. BabyJubJub and Poseidon are `BigInt`, as the README says.
3. **Storage.** `#record` draws 12 bytes for each write (`keys.js` l. 238); the key is
   PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds, a 16-byte salt, new at each change of passphrase; `exportBackup`
   throws without a key; a clear backup restored with a passphrase is encrypted with it; a wrong
   passphrase fails the tag. Left as `docs/AUDIT.md` §8.4 says: the passphrase is optional. One
   thing more: restoring from the words with the passphrase field empty stores them in clear without
   the question that creation asks (`ui/vault-panel.js` l. 120, against l. 100); the panel then
   shows its warning.
4. **Lock.** `lock()` zeroes the master secret, the viewing key and the lock secrets and drops the
   rest; every lock goes through `lockVault`, which stops the prover; the witness is zeroed after
   each proof (`ui/spends.js` l. 64). The stale copy: vault-7.
5. **Counters.** `reserveNote` and `reserveLock` save before they return; a MINT reserves after the
   wallet signed and before the broadcast, and stops if the index moved (`ui/tokens-panel.js`
   l. 198); a restored vault throws from both until `chainChecked` (`keys.js` l. 291), which the
   page calls only after every leaf was read with no indexer disagreeing and every indexer's notes
   and listings were read. Two copies at once: vault-7.
6. **Addresses.** `planTransfer` decodes with `decodeAddress` and the page's prefix (`send.js`
   l. 54); the function is `circuit/js`'s, with the low-order check on `IVK`.
7. **Outputs.** `seal` gets 32 fresh bytes for each output (`spend.js` l. 99); `shuffled` draws
   from `crypto.getRandomValues`; the change and the unused outputs go to the vault's address; an
   unused input has two random scalars and a random `rho` (l. 40). No `Math.random` in `vault/src`.
8. **Scanning.** `scan` returns null for a malformed output, a low-order `pk_eph` (decided from its
   bytes in `subtle.js`), a failed tag, a plaintext that names no ticker, or a leaf that is not the
   one computed; anything thrown propagates, and `matchNotes` keeps the sync's leaves unscanned from
   the first that failed. `receive` drops a note of 0. Notes are `out:<pos>`. The one case found:
   vault-9. What a scan's duration tells an indexer, and the five `pk_eph` Firefox refuses, are
   sealing-1 and sealing-2 of the sealing audit; this part did not look there.
9. **Public notes.** `matchPublic` recomputes the leaf from `(ticker, v, npk, 0)` and requires the
   leaf the sync put at that position; `sync` refuses a list with a gap and a tree whose root is not
   the indexer's own. A second note under one `npk` gets an id of its own.
10. **Questions.** `IndexerApi.listing` is called from `listingCheck` alone, for an offer to buy;
    own listings come from the full list; no source names `/path/` or `/nullifier/` (the test of
    `test/page.test.js`, and the regtest run checks every request). A BURN or a LIST asks
    `/token/<ticker>` first, which names the ticker to the indexers before the transaction shows it;
    a TRANSFER asks nothing of the kind; a DEPLOY's ticker is looked for in the full `/tokens`.
11. **Spends.** `buildSpend` opens each input again (`spend.js` l. 85), checks the sum, signs the
    hash of every byte before the proof, verifies each signature; `withProof` puts the proof in the
    same envelope object. The regtest run has Bitcoin Core and two indexers accept one of each kind.
12. **Proofs.** `proveWithFreshSeed` draws the seed in the worker for each proof; `proveSpend`
    verifies before the carrier is planned; the verifying key's digest is compared with the pinned
    one at each load (`prover.js` l. 106). Since #92 the module hashes the seed with the witness
    before it seeds its generator, and the host refuses a witness, a seed, a proof or public inputs
    of another size (verifier-3 and verifier-1 of the verifier audit).
13. **Anchor.** The root is the local tree's at the synced height, which `sync` compared with the
    indexer's; `anchor` refuses a tree more than 90 blocks old or less than `K_min` deep. Its checks
    need a tip: vault-12. The terms of a DEPLOY at the block it lands in: vault-4. What the height
    of an anchor shows in public is sealing-9.
14. **Reveal key.** `addPending` saves the one-time key before `signFunded` (`ui/spends.js` l. 76,
    l. 77). Notes given back after a refusal need every indexer (`refusalAgreed`). A spend that is
    never mined: vault-2.
15. **The wallet.** Every PSBT is built by `buildFunded`; `signFunded` and the mint compare the
    returned transaction with `sameUnsignedTx` (version, inputs, sequences, outputs, locktime) and
    stop on a difference. Inputs are P2WPKH, P2SH-P2WPKH or P2TR only (`mint.js` l. 116), so no
    third party changes a commit's txid, and the reveal is signed for the txid of the transaction as
    signed, script included.
16. **Signatures.** `bip340Sign` was read against BIP340's default signing and `sigMsg` against
    BIP341's message for the two modes used, key path and script path; `tweakSecret` against
    `taproot_tweak_seckey`. A lock key signs three things: the BIP341 hash of an offer, that of a
    cancel, and `SHA256("sh20/v0/claim" ‖ listTxid ‖ npk)` with a note key of the vault; a one-time
    key signs its reveal and, tweaked, a sweep. Each message is built by the vault from its own
    record or from a transaction whose txid it recomputed. A wallet chooses its address, its public
    key and its coins: a wrong one gives a transaction the network refuses, signed for nobody else.
    `test/secp256k1.test.js`, `test/taproot.test.js` (the vectors of both BIPs).
17. **One envelope.** A MINT, a TAKE, a cancel and a CLAIM have one `OP_RETURN` with the envelope;
    their input 0 is a key-path or a P2WPKH witness, which `tapscriptOf` does not read as a script
    path (one element, or a last element that starts with `02` or `03`); a TAKE's payee must be one
    of five standard templates, none of which is an `OP_RETURN`. A reveal has its one tapscript.
18. **Fees.** `Esplora.feeRate` clamps to [1, 200]; `buildFunded` refuses above 1,000; a fee above
    20,000 sats is asked about in `signFunded` and at a mint. When no change is left, the remainder
    is below the fee of a change output plus its dust limit.
19. **Lock.** `planList` puts `lockVout = 0` and the lock's key in the body; the reveal's only
    output is that lock, 330 sats; the key is `HMAC-SHA512("sh20/vault/lock", m ‖ u32le(i))` reduced
    into `[1, n − 1]`, under BIP86.
20. **Offers.** `offerSkeleton` fixes the version, the locktime, the outpoint, its amount and
    script, the sequence and output 0. `offerTerms` signs its own record for a listing it sent and
    refuses any difference; after a restore, a proof with one of the vault's nullifiers. That path
    is sound whatever root the indexer gave: a nullifier is `Poseidon(nk, leaf, pos)`, so a proof
    that carries the vault's needs the vault's `nk` and signature, and a wrong root can only make it
    fail. Left, as §8.2 says: the dialog. vault-6 would remove it.
21. **Buying.** `buildTake` checks the seller's signature under the key of the lock output, which
    the txid covers, for the price and payee it will pay; both come from the chain and from two
    indexers that agree, and are checked again when Buy is pressed. The seller is paid by any
    transaction that uses the signature. What the buyer relies on beyond that: vault-1, vault-13.
22. **CLAIM.** `buildClaim` signs the record's `listTxid` and a note key reserved from the vault;
    every indexer must say CLOSED, and the chain must show the lock spent by a transaction with no
    TAKE.
23. **Profile.** `profile.js` takes its constants from `indexer/src/constants.js`; `/status` is
    compared with them and never read into them; `requireProfile` runs before each action and asks
    again.
24. **Checks against the chain.** For a mint: `crossCheck` across indexers, then the DEPLOY or the
    genesis constants. For a listing: the transaction and every indexer. Gaps: vault-5, vault-6,
    and vault-16 for an indexer that verifies no spend.
25. **Robustness and the DOM.** No `innerHTML`, `outerHTML`, `insertAdjacentHTML`, `document.write`,
    `eval` or `Function` in `vault/src` (searched); `h()` appends strings as text, and the one
    attribute built from a server's value is a link's `href`, made of the saved Esplora address and
    an id. Amounts typed are decimal digits, and the codec refuses a value of 2^64 or more
    (checked). What stops the page: vault-3, vault-10.
26. **Scripts.** The policy allows the page's own scripts, the import map by hash, and extension
    schemes; no inline script or style, no `javascript:` link; `connect-src` is the page, `https:`
    and the local host. The page stops in a frame and on a path gateway before it reads a setting;
    servers in a link are named in a `confirm`, for that tab, with a warning that stays.
27. **Service worker.** Registered on a subdomain gateway only, from the site's root; `sw.js` adds
    three headers to each response and keeps nothing. vault-11 for the Pages site.
28. **Release.** `release.js` reads the commit's objects, `site()` lists the files and refuses an
    import that leaves them; `vault/tools/prover.sh --check` rebuilt the two modules and found the
    committed bytes, at both commits (#92 changed them in between). For signet; vault-17 for
    mainnet.
29. **Addresses of the profile's network.** `btc.js` has Bitcoin's own constants for mainnet (`bc`,
    0x00, 0x05) and reads and writes every address with the profile's (`NETWORK`, l. 14); the SH-20
    prefix is `HRP[CHAIN.addresses]` (`notekeys.js` l. 19), used for the vault's address and for
    every address it pays (`send.js` l. 54). No other place reads an address: the payee of a LIST
    and of a DEPLOY, the wallet's own and its second one all go through `addressToScript`. An
    address of the other network is refused each time.
30. **Wallets.** Each adapter asks for the profile's network and switches Xverse and UniSat to it.
    What holds a wallet to it is the address: `renderWallet` refuses one that is not of the network,
    and nothing decides on the `network` an adapter reports. Signet and testnet share a prefix, as
    before. Read against the adapters, not run with a wallet.
31. **One network for a vault.** `fromWords` records `CHAIN.bitcoin`, and `open` refuses another
    before any key is derived, for the stored record and for a backup; a record with no network is
    signet's. The way from one network to the other: vault-14.
32. **No indexer.** The mainnet profile has none by default. `refresh` and `getSyncer` say so and

## 5. Open

Suspected and not settled:

- **A refusal that does not last.** `refusedSpend` gives notes back six blocks after a refused
  reveal, while the envelope is public and its anchor may still be in the window. A refusal by the
  turnstile can turn into an acceptance when the pool grows, in a copy of the envelope that anyone
  publishes; a user who sent the BURN again from other notes would then burn twice. The rule
  proposed for vault-2 (wait until the anchor is out of the window) would cover it. Not reproduced.
- **Two saves under way in one tab.** `save` snapshots the state, awaits the encryption, then
  writes. If two encryptions finish out of order, the older state is the one stored until the next
  save. Not observed; browsers seem to answer in order.
- **Timing.** `secp256k1.js` was read for what `docs/AUDIT.md` §8.7 says of it, not measured again.

Not covered:

- A mainnet page as a user meets it: with a wallet extension set to mainnet, with coins that carry
  something (vault-15 is read from the code and from where outputs stand in a transaction, not
  from a wallet), at a fee rate the network asks for.
- `vault/dev/before-a.js` and the tests that came with #84, #85 and #93, which were run and not
  read line by line.
- `circuit/js/address.js`, `seal.js`, `babyjub.js` and `notes.js`, which belong to the part
  `sealing`; `indexer/src/codec.js`, `tree.js`, `schnorr.js`, `poseidon.js`, which belong to the
  part `indexer`; `circuit/web/host.js` and the two WebAssembly modules, beyond the rebuild.
- `vault/tools/pack.js`, the `release` workflow, `vault/dev/`, and `relayer/`, which no part of the
  prompt names.
- Real wallets. Everything about Xverse, UniSat and Leather was read against the adapters, not run:
  whether they sign a PSBT that carries an input already final (a TAKE, a cancel, a sweep), what
  they do with an unconfirmed coin, and whether they inject their provider into an isolated page.
- Safari and Firefox: the regtest run is in Chrome.
